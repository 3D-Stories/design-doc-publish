The shape this repository forces —

A style cannot land incrementally. Adding minutes to MODULES turns seven guard files red at once — the golden SHA oracle, the classification test, the two doc-table pins, the gallery guard, the currency gate, the rendered-styles guard and the reachability guard.

That is by design. Each guard exists so a style cannot ship with a surface missing. So this is one red-to-green arc with a scoped verification per task, and the full suite runs once at Step 9.

No task declares a parallel_group. Each one depends on the registration from the task before it, so the whole plan is serial and says so rather than claiming a parallelism it cannot prove.

Three files are touched by more than one task, and that is intended rather than a conflict. scripts/tests/regen_docs_pages.py is edited three times (T0's stamp bump, T6's gallery entry, T9's two planning entries) and README.md twice (T4's --type row, T6's gallery cell). The files declarations exist so a parallel_group can be judged disjoint. Nothing here is parallel, so the overlap costs nothing — but one writer at a time is still the rule, and the serial order above is that writer.

What the screenshot assertions do and do not prove

Both review passes raised the same objection and it is fair. Correct dimensions plus a vague "non-trivial" size do not establish that the PNG depicts the minutes page. A browser error page is 1280 x 1000 too.

Declined: a reviewed SHA oracle or an image-variance comparison. That was the recommended remedy. A screenshot SHA is brittle across font rendering, Chrome versions and GPU flags, so it would fail for reasons having nothing to do with the page. This repository also carries no image-comparison dependency to set a tolerance against. A gate that goes red on a Chrome upgrade teaches people to ignore it.

Adopted instead, two things, and neither pretends to be the other:

  1. A byte floor DERIVED from committed data, not invented. The smallest committed gallery PNG is module-map.png at 72527 bytes, so the floor is half of that. It is self-maintaining, and it catches a blank page, which compresses to a few kilobytes.
  2. A recorded visual inspection. The captured PNG is opened and looked at, and what was seen goes into the session notes: the minutes heading, the ledger strip, and at least the agreed and decided regions.

The automated assertions bound the failure class. The human look is the evidence. That is stated plainly because the reverse claim would be the defect: no assertion in this plan proves the screenshot depicts minutes. The inspection does, once, at the commit that creates it. A later silent rot of that PNG is NOT caught, and #58 already recorded the same gap for the other fourteen.
